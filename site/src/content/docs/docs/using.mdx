`@webvpn/client` opens a session with a relay, and gives a page TCP connections, listening ports, UDP sockets, name lookups and IP tunnels as Web Streams. `@webvpn/net` and `@webvpn/dgram` put Node's `net` and `dgram` modules on a session, so code written for Node runs in a page.

## Get the packages

```sh
npm install @webvpn/client
```

`@webvpn/net` and `@webvpn/dgram` are packages of their own, for code written for Node's `net` and `dgram`:

```sh
npm install @webvpn/net @webvpn/dgram
```

Each package ships its JavaScript as an ES module, with its types.

## Connect to a relay

`connect(options)` opens a session with a relay. It resolves with the session once the relay and the client have exchanged their hellos.

<Code code={connectExample} lang="ts" />

- `url` is the relay's WebTransport URL, and `webSocketUrl` its WebSocket URL. A [`webvpnd`](/docs/running-a-relay/#ports) relay on its default ports takes `https://<host>:4433` and `wss://<host>:8443`.
- `transport` picks the carrier. `auto`, the default, takes WebTransport when the browser has it and `url` is given. It takes the WebSocket when either is missing, or when the WebTransport session does not open. `webtransport` and `websocket` take that carrier only. `session.carrier` names the carrier the session uses.
- `webTransportTimeoutMs` is how long WebTransport may take to be ready, 4000 by default.
- `agent` is the client's name in its hello, `@webvpn/client` by default.

`connect` rejects with a `TypeError` when the options give no URL for the carrier it takes. Otherwise it rejects with the browser's own error, or with a `WebVpnError`. The `code` of a `WebVpnError` is one of the protocol's [error codes](/docs/protocol/#error-codes). When the relay closed the session in its setup, its `closeCode` says why, such as `SESSION_LIMIT` for a relay that holds as many sessions as it allows. `ErrorCode` and `CloseCode` name every code.

### A credential

A relay may require a credential. Until the client presents one, the relay answers its socket requests and its lookups with `AUTH_REQUIRED`. The `credential` option presents one right after the hello, as bytes. For `webvpnd`, a credential is one entry of [`WEBVPN_CREDENTIALS`](/docs/running-a-relay/#credentials), and the page presents its UTF-8 bytes, `new TextEncoder().encode(text)`. When the relay refuses the credential, `connect` closes the session and rejects with `AUTH_FAILED`.

`session.authenticate(credential)` presents a credential later, for example a renewed one, and `session.clearAuth()` gives it up. A refused credential leaves the session as it was.

`session.features` holds the bits of the relay's hello: `Feature.LISTEN`, `Feature.DNS`, `Feature.AUTH`, `Feature.AUTH_REQUIRED` and `Feature.IP`. A call that needs a feature the relay does not offer rejects with `UNSUPPORTED`, and the client sends nothing.

## Open a TCP connection

`session.tcpConnect(target)` opens a TCP connection, and resolves once the relay has opened it. The target is an address, `{ ip, port }`, or a name, `{ name, port }`. The relay looks a name up and connects to its first IPv4 address, in the same round trip.

<Code code={tcpExample} lang="ts" />

- `readable` carries the peer's bytes, and ends when the peer finished its side.
- `writable` carries bytes to the peer. Closing it is a half close: the peer reads the end, and `readable` stays open.
- `close()` ends the connection in order, and `reset()` ends it with a reset toward the peer. After either, `readable` ends.
- `setOption(option)` sets an option, such as `{ type: 'noDelay', value: true }`: `noDelay`, `ttl`, `keepAlive`, `sendBufferSize` or `recvBufferSize`.
- `local` is the relay's end of the connection, and `remote` the peer's.

A connection that does not open rejects with a `WebVpnError`, such as `CONNECTION_REFUSED`, `TIMED_OUT`, or `REFUSED` for a target the relay's policy refuses. A peer that resets the connection errors `readable` with `CONNECTION_RESET`.

An address's `ip` holds 4 bytes for IPv4 or 16 for IPv6. `parseIp(text)` reads an address's text into those bytes, and returns `undefined` for anything else, such as a name. `formatIp(ip)` writes the text back. A name needs the relay's `DNS` feature, which `webvpnd` has when `WEBVPN_DNS_UPSTREAM` is set.

## Listen on a port

`session.tcpListen(local)` takes a port on the relay. Port `0` takes a free port, and `listener.local.port` is the port the relay took. Other computers connect to the relay's address on that port, and each connection arrives on `listener.connections` as a TCP socket. The example sends back what each connection sends.

<Code code={listenExample} lang="ts" />

`{ ip: new Uint8Array(4), port: 0 }` is `0.0.0.0` with a free port: every IPv4 address of the relay. A listener needs the relay's `LISTEN` feature. `webvpnd` binds ports from 1024 up, and refuses a lower one with `REFUSED`. `listener.close()` stops listening, and `connections` ends.

## Use UDP

`session.udpBind(local)` binds a UDP socket on the relay. Port `0` takes a free port. Other computers send to the relay's address on `socket.local.port`.

<Code code={udpExample} lang="ts" />

- `writable` sends each `{ remote, data }` written to it as one datagram. It never waits: it drops a datagram the carrier cannot take at once, or one over 65536 bytes, as a network would.
- `readable` gives each datagram the socket received, as `{ remote, data }`. Datagrams wait for the reader up to 4 MiB for the socket and 32 MiB for the session. Past that, they are dropped.
- `connect(remote)` fixes the peer: a datagram written with no `remote` goes to it, and only its datagrams arrive. `disconnect()` clears it.
- `setOption(option)` sets the broadcast flag, the TTLs, the multicast loopback and memberships, and the buffer sizes.
- `close()` closes the socket, and `readable` ends. Cancelling `readable` closes the socket too.

Over WebTransport, a socket's datagrams travel as WebTransport datagrams until the socket's stream is in place, and a browser may drop those.

## Look names up

`session.lookup(query)` sends a whole DNS message to the relay. The relay asks its resolver, and returns the resolver's whole answer. `dnsQuery(name, type)` builds the query, for `A` records, the default, or `AAAA` records. `dnsAddresses(answer)` reads the addresses in the answer.

<Code code={lookupExample} lang="ts" />

A name with no records is an answer with no address, not an error. A name outside ASCII goes in its `xn--` form. Lookups need the relay's `DNS` feature, and a query holds at most 512 bytes. The resolver sees the relay's address, not the person's.

## IP tunnels

An IP tunnel carries whole IP packets, for a program that brings its own network stack, such as a virtual machine running in the page. The relay forwards each packet the program sends as a router does, and sends back the packets that arrive for the tunnel's addresses. Only a relay that offers tunnels takes them: its hello sets `Feature.IP`, and on any other relay `session.ipOpen()` rejects with `UNSUPPORTED`. A relay offers them when the program that runs it gives it a [forwarder](/docs/embedding/#ip-tunnels).

`session.ipOpen()` opens a tunnel. The example hands its packets to a network stack in a worker, and sends back the packets the stack gives.

<Code code={ipTunnelExample} lang="ts" />

- `ipv4` and `ipv6` are the addresses the relay gave this end of the tunnel, as text. A tunnel carries at least one of the two families, and the address of a family it does not carry is absent.
- `mtu` is the largest packet the tunnel carries, at least 1280 bytes.
- `carrier` is the carrier the tunnel's packets ride, `'webtransport'` or `'websocket'`, as `session.carrier` says.
- `ports` is how many public ports the relay gave the tunnel, from `firstPort` on, and `publicIpv4` and `publicIpv6` are the relay's addresses as others dial them. A TCP or UDP packet anyone outside the relay sends to a public address on one of those ports reaches the tunnel's own address on the same port. A client of the same relay does not reach them. With `ports` at 0, the tunnel has none, and only answers to what it sent reach it.
- `readable` gives each packet from the relay, one whole IPv4 or IPv6 packet per chunk, with no link header. Each chunk is a `Uint8Array` with a buffer of its own, so it moves to a worker without a copy. The client reads the tunnel only as fast as the page reads `readable`: a page that stops reading makes the relay drop packets.
- `send(packet)` sends one packet and never waits. It returns `false` when it dropped the packet: an empty one, one longer than `mtu`, one on a tunnel that stopped, or one that found the queue toward the relay full. The queue holds 256 KiB, which a slow link takes long to carry: about 0.2 seconds at 10 Mbit/s and 2 seconds at 1 Mbit/s, for every packet behind it. The browser, over WebTransport, and the system, over the WebSocket, hold more below that queue, where a page cannot reach. A dropped packet is lost, as on a busy link, and the protocols on top send again as they do on any network. Over the WebSocket, the queue also holds what the session's other sockets have not sent yet, so the tunnel drops every packet while one of them uploads.
- `close()` closes the tunnel, and `readable` ends. Cancelling `readable` closes the tunnel too. `closed` resolves with who stopped it: the page, the relay, or the end of the session.

A tunnel has two ends and nothing between them, so it has no gateway address. A stack that wants one makes one up, and sends every packet into the tunnel. The client does not look into a packet. The relay drops, and says nothing about, a packet it does not forward, such as one whose source is not the tunnel's address or whose destination its rules refuse. Packets may be dropped on the way, as on any link, and the tunnel sends nothing again.

### Sockets over a tunnel

`openStack(tunnel, options)`, from `@webvpn/client/stack`, runs a TCP/IP stack in the page on a tunnel, and gives the socket calls of a session: `tcpConnect`, `tcpListen`, `udpBind` and `lookup`, with the same arguments, results and errors. Code written for a session's sockets runs on it unchanged, and `createNet(stack)` and `createDgram(stack)` take it as they take a session. The stack is [smoltcp](https://github.com/smoltcp-rs/smoltcp) built to WebAssembly, about 60 KB compressed, and only a page that imports `@webvpn/client/stack` carries it. The relay must offer tunnels, as above.

<Code code={ipStackExample} lang="ts" />

What differs from a session's sockets:

- TCP runs in the page, and the relay only forwards packets. The page's stack sends again what the tunnel lost, and paces itself on that loss: a packet that `send` drops counts as one lost on the way. smoltcp has no selective acknowledgements, and waits at least a second before it sends a lost packet again when three duplicate acknowledgements did not show it, so a tunnel that drops many packets is slow.
- Over the WebSocket, a connection on the stack stalls while a socket of the session uploads, since the tunnel then drops every packet. After 15 minutes of that, a connection whose sent bytes wait for the peer fails with `CONNECTION_RESET`, as below.
- `tcpListen` takes one of the tunnel's public ports. Port `0` takes a free one, any other port must be one of them, or the call rejects with `ADDRESS_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and a tunnel with no public ports rejects with `UNSUPPORTED`. `listener.local` is the relay's public address and the port, which is what a peer dials. A listener holds at most `options.backlog` connections, 8 by default, that are opening or that `connections` has not handed out, and refuses one more with a reset, where a session's listener keeps taking them. A connection the peer has not finished opening 10 seconds after its first packet is reset, and gives its place in the backlog back.
- The stack ends a connection whose peer stopped answering as Linux on the relay does: after 15 minutes of silence while sent bytes or the end wait for the peer's acknowledgement, and, with the `keepAlive` option on, after the idle time and then 9 probes 75 seconds apart. `readable` and the writes then fail with `CONNECTION_RESET`, as a session's do. Its probes go out every 75 seconds of idle time, or every `initialDelaySeconds` when that is shorter. A connection without `keepAlive` stays open however long it is idle.
- `udpBind` on port `0` takes a free public port while the tunnel has one, so that anyone outside the relay can reach the socket, and a private port otherwise, which only answers reach. `socket.local` says which: the public address, or the tunnel's own.
- `lookup` asks a resolver through the tunnel, `1.1.1.1` unless `options.resolver` names another. It asks again once when no answer came, asks over TCP when the answer is truncated, and rejects with `LOOKUP_FAILED` after 5 seconds. A connect to a name looks it up the same way, and takes its first A record, or its first AAAA record on a tunnel without IPv4.
- A socket's buffers are fixed when it is made. By default it has 512 KiB to receive over WebTransport and 128 KiB over the WebSocket, and 128 KiB to send. The receive buffer is the most a connection's window offers, so a connection carries about 40 Mbit/s at a 100 ms round trip over WebTransport, and about 10 Mbit/s over the WebSocket. A TCP socket takes both buffers in the page's memory when it is made, 640 KiB by default over WebTransport and 256 KiB over the WebSocket, and a listener takes `backlog` of them at once. A closed socket keeps them until its connection ends, a minute at most. The stack uses freed memory again but gives none back to the page until it closes, so 100 sockets open at once hold about 64 MiB over WebTransport. `options.tcpReceiveBuffer`, `tcpSendBuffer`, `udpReceiveBuffer` and `udpSendBuffer` change them for the sockets made after, and the `sendBufferSize` and `recvBufferSize` options reject with `UNSUPPORTED`, as do UDP's broadcast and multicast options.
- A UDP datagram must fit the tunnel's `mtu` with its headers, since the stack does not fragment. A larger one fails its write.

The stack reads the tunnel and sends on it from then on, so nothing else may. `stack.close()` closes the tunnel. When the tunnel ends, every socket on it ends as a session's sockets do when the session ends, and `stack.closed` resolves with who stopped the tunnel. If the stack itself fails, for example when the page runs out of memory for its sockets, it closes the tunnel, and `stack.closed` resolves with `{ by: 'client' }`, as after `stack.close()`. The sockets then end as when the tunnel ends, and their errors have the code `OTHER` and the message 'the stack failed'.

## When the session ends

`session.draining` resolves when the relay says it is about to stop. The session's sockets keep working, and new sockets belong on a new session. `session.closed` resolves once the session ended, with a close code and a reason for a person. Every socket on the session has ended then, and every request on it rejects.

<Code code={sessionEndExample} lang="ts" />

The codes a page reads in `closed`:

| Code | Name | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `NORMAL` | The page closed the session with `session.close()`. |
| 1 | `PROTOCOL_ERROR` | A side could not read a frame, or the relay answered nothing. |
| 4 | `RESTARTING` | The relay stopped. A new session may open at once. |
| 5 | `LOST` | The connection ended with no code, as a lost network does. |

A session that ends in its setup makes `connect` reject instead, with the code in `closeCode`: `SESSION_LIMIT` for a relay that holds as many sessions as it allows, `VERSION_MISMATCH` for a relay whose version does not suit, and `RESTARTING` for a relay that is stopping. [Closing a session](/docs/protocol/#closing-a-session) lists every code.

The module-level exports, such as `connect` and `createServer` of `@webvpn/net` and `createSocket` of `@webvpn/dgram`, use the session given to `setProvider(session)`. Each module has its own `setProvider`. Until it is called, a connect, a listen, a bind or a send throws.

## What the client does not do

- **It picks no relay.** `connect` opens a session with the relay its URLs name.
- **It opens no session on its own.** A session that ended stays closed, and the page calls `connect` again.
- **It keeps nothing between sessions.** A new session holds no socket and no credential of an earlier one.
- **It shares no session.** Each page or worker that calls `connect` holds a session of its own.
- **It retries no request.** A request the relay does not answer in 10 seconds, or 12 for a connection, rejects with `TIMED_OUT`.
- **It caches no lookup.** Each lookup, and each connection to a name, asks the relay.
